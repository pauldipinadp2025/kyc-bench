SECTION 01

Compliance is the gap in existing evals

Most AI benchmarks test investment banking, corporate law, or software engineering. Compliance review — the work banks do to check who a customer is and whether their activity looks suspicious — is not on that list, even though it's high-volume, expert-heavy work.

It's also a genuinely hard format for an agent. A single case means reading several documents, tracing ownership through a registry, screening names against a watchlist, and writing a note an auditor could follow. And mistakes aren't equal — missing a sanctions match costs far more than a false alarm, which plain accuracy scores hide.

SECTION 02

What one case looks like

  1. The agent gets a case file — a company applying to open an account, with its own documents.
  2. It looks up the company in a mock registry tool and finds a 60% shareholder.
  3. It keeps tracing — that shareholder is itself owned by another entity.
  4. It finds the trap — the shares are held by a nominee on behalf of an undisclosed owner.
  5. It screens that owner's name and gets a watchlist match.
  6. It decides — hold for Enhanced Due Diligence, cite the rule, and write a note an auditor could follow.
SECTION 03

Errors are weighted by consequence

Approving a case that needed Enhanced Due Diligence costs far more than sending a clean case to EDD by mistake. The scoring reflects that asymmetry directly:

There's also a critical-miss rule: if the agent misses the one piece of evidence that mattered most — the hidden PEP, the nominee filing — its total score is capped at 0.40, however good the rest of its answer reads.

SECTION 04

What's in the repository

cases/What the agent sees — documents, watchlist, company registry.
answers/The correct answer for each case, kept out of the agent's view.
policy/A short, invented bank policy every case is graded against.
kycbench/Scoring logic, mock tools, and a small command-line tool.
docs/Design notes and the rubric used to grade the written file note.
